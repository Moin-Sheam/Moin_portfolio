200–1,000 m · mesopelagic

Working at the interface of microbiology, oceanography, and computation.

I'm a PhD candidate in the Department of Biological Sciences at UNC Charlotte, working in Dr. Elaine Luo's Microbial Ecology Lab. My dissertation examines the biogeochemical and ecological impact of viruses in aquatic environments — from the sunlit surface ocean down to hydrothermal vent fluids on the seafloor. Before Charlotte, I completed an MSc in Marine Biology at Ghent University, Belgium, and a BSc in Biotechnology & Genetic Engineering at Islamic University, Bangladesh, where my early work centered on bioremediation and environmental microbiology.

1,000–4,000 m · bathypelagic

Tracing viruses through the water column, from open ocean to vent fluid.

Three questions run through my current work: where do marine viruses come from, how far do they travel, and what do they do to the microbes — and the carbon and energy budgets — around them.

North Pacific Subtropical Gyre · 5–4,000 m

Giant viruses on the move

Using paired planktonic and particle-associated metagenomes, I track how nucleocytoplasmic large DNA viruses ("giant viruses") are transported vertically through the water column, and how their auxiliary metabolic genes intersect with the marine carbon cycle.

Global hydrothermal vents · 514 samples

Vent virus biogeography

A large-scale metagenomic survey of giant viruses across globally distributed hydrothermal vents and the surrounding pelagic ocean, testing whether vent-derived viral populations follow an "everything is everywhere" distribution — and where heat-shock-encoding viruses are selected for instead.

qSIP-informed metagenomics

Viruses infecting chemoautotrophs

Collaborative work using quantitative stable isotope probing to identify which viruses are actively infecting chemoautotrophic hosts — the microbes that turn dissolved inorganic carbon into biomass at the base of vent and deep-sea food webs.
